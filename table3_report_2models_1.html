The rule is in there.

C2 asks where a newly learned synthetic concept appears inside a transformer. We read activations from every layer while a model processes k demonstrations of a hidden rule, then train a linear probe to recover the correct answer for a held-out query. In two unrelated model families, the probe succeeds on concepts and rule types it has never seen while the model itself performs near chance.

Evidence for in-context acquisition

The representation sharpens with every demonstration. Behaviour doesn't.

A static property of the prompt format would not depend on how many examples the model has seen. Probe accuracy climbs monotonically with k in both models, while the model's own answers stay near chance until the very top of the range. The shaded band is the gap.

Control · Family holdout

It transfers to rule types it never trained on

The transfer split holds out concepts but not rule families, so a probe could be recognising "what conjunctive rules look like" rather than abstracting. Training on three families and testing on the fourth removes that route. No fold collapses in either model, and the per-family ordering is nearly identical.

What this does to H2

H2 predicts internal mechanisms that directly mediate concept generalization. There is no behavioural generalization here for a mechanism to mediate — the representation forms, transfers across rule types, and the readout fails.

That is a stranger claim than a confirmation, and arguably a more interesting one, but the paper's framing has to say so. The honest statement is representation without readout, at these scales, on this task.

The undefined L_S may be the mechanism: in both models the representation peaks mid-network and decays toward the output. Table 4's causal ablation at the peak layer would test whether the mid-network encoding is connected to the weak 16-shot behaviour at all.

Methodology · Control that changed the result

The first probe was reading the prompt, not the concept

An earlier run on Llama-3-8B probed concept identity — which of 20 concepts an activation came from. It returned 5.0% at layer 0 and 100% flat across layers 1–24.

Diagnostic signature

Each concept's prompt contains its own distinctive nonce tokens, so one attention layer suffices to separate prompts at the final position, and the residual stream carries that signature forward unchanged. The same 100% would appear with unlearnable demonstrations.

A step function to ceiling at layer 1 means information present in the input. A genuine emergent representation rises mid-network, peaks below ceiling, and often decays — which is what the corrected probe produces in both models reported here.

Fix: probe the query's ground-truth label under a cross-concept split, so token identity cannot transfer. The permutation null was clean in the original run too (4.5% against 5% nominal), confirming the problem was the probe target rather than overfitting.

Limitations

What these runs cannot support

Two models, both small

1.5B and 2B. The dissociation replicates across families but not yet across scale. A 7B+ point is needed before generalising.

No real-word control

Near-chance behaviour cannot yet be separated from "this task is unlearnable for anyone." Same rules over English tokens is the needed comparison.

No human baseline

"Human-like" is in the project title with no humans in the experiments. Inducing a hidden 2-character marker from 4 examples may be hard for people too.

Linear probes only

A concept distributed non-linearly would be invisible to this method. Absence of signal is not evidence of absence.

Asymmetric class tokens

Gemma splits the two class names unevenly (' blick' vs ' d'), which could bias the logit comparison independently of the rule.

Disjunction anomaly

Disjunctive rules underperform in both models despite being logically simpler than negation. Could be real, could be item generation. Unresolved.

Next

Three things that would settle it

Real-word control — same rules, English tokens. Separates model limitation from task impossibility. Cheapest of the three.

Scale — a 7B+ model. Does behaviour catch up to the probe, or does the gap persist? Note the gap is wider in the better model here, which is not the naive prediction.

Causal validation (Table 4) — ablate the peak-layer representation and test whether the weak 16-shot behavioural signal degrades. That is the difference between correlation and mechanism.
